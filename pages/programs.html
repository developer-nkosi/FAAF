What We Do

Five integrated program pillars

FAAF operates through interconnected programs that build skills, create mentorships, and empower communities to sustain their own development.

Pillar 1

Skills Development

We deliver market-responsive technical and soft skills training that converts unemployed individuals into productive workers and entrepreneurs.

  • Vocational Training: Construction trades, agribusiness, digital skills, hospitality, renewable energy installation
  • Soft Skills Integration: Communication, financial literacy, problem-solving, professionalism embedded in all technical programs
  • Market Linkage: Direct connection to employers, apprenticeship placements, and cooperative formation
  • Competency-Based Certification: Graduates demonstrate skills, not just attend classes
01

Skills Development

Building market-ready capabilities for sustainable employment

Pillar 2

Mentorship Programs

We build relational infrastructure that accelerates growth, prevents costly mistakes, and transmits values alongside skills.

  • Professional Mentorship: One-to-one matching with employed professionals for career guidance
  • Peer Mentorship: Alumni mentor current participants, creating cascading impact
  • Young Boys' Mentorship: Specialized program addressing the crisis of male role model absence through structured group and individual mentoring in positive masculinity, behavioral development, education retention, and violence prevention
  • Women's Mentorship Circles: Peer-based business and leadership support for female entrepreneurs
02

Mentorship Programs

Relational infrastructure for growth and values transmission

Pillar 3

Entrepreneurship & Economic Empowerment

We convert skills into sustainable enterprises through capital, coaching, and market access.

  • Startup Support: Seed grants for ultra-poor participants; micro-loans for others; business plan competitions
  • Business Development Services: Registration, bookkeeping, marketing, digital commerce training
  • Cooperative Formation: Collective bargaining power and shared infrastructure
  • Incubation Spaces: Shared equipment and workspace for early-stage businesses
03

Economic Empowerment

Turning skills into sustainable enterprises

Pillar 4

Youth & Child Empowerment

We invest early to prevent dependency before it takes root.

  • Youth Leadership Academies: Civic engagement, public speaking, project management, advocacy skills
  • Child Protection: Safe spaces, safeguarding protocols, psychosocial support, educational retention programs
  • Adolescent Girls' Programs: Menstrual health management, school retention, early marriage prevention
  • Early Skills Exposure: Age-appropriate vocational curiosity programs for children
04

Youth & Child Empowerment

Investing early to prevent dependency

Pillar 5

Community Development & Institutional Strengthening

We build community capacity to manage its own development trajectory.

  • Community Development Committees: Governance training, participatory planning, local resource mobilization
  • Infrastructure Support: Community-managed projects with matching contributions and maintenance training
  • Knowledge Exchange: Inter-community learning, documentation of best practices, replication support
  • Graduation Protocols: Structured transition from FAAF implementation to community ownership
05

Institutional Strengthening

Building communities that manage their own future

Who We Serve

Priority beneficiary groups

FAAF prioritizes individuals and communities facing structural barriers to self-reliance.

Beneficiary Group Specific Focus Rationale
Youth (15-30) Unemployed, underemployed, school dropouts, rural-urban migrants Malawi's demographic dividend is youth; without investment, it becomes a liability
Women (18-55) Single heads of household, survivors of gender-based violence, economically excluded by cultural norms Women's economic empowerment correlates directly with child nutrition, education, and health outcomes
Children (3-17) Orphans, child laborers, street-connected children, those in child-headed households Early intervention prevents adult dependency; protection is non-negotiable
Vulnerable Individuals Persons with disabilities, refugees/IDPs, elderly without support, chronically ill Standard programs exclude these groups; adaptive design is required
Young Boys (10-17) Fatherless, in single-mother households, at-risk of school dropout, exposed to negative peer influence The mentorship gap contributes to violence, unemployment, and perpetuation of poverty cycles
