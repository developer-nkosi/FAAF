Who We Are

Our Story

FAAF is a non-profit, community-based organization in Malawi that transforms the development paradigm by investing in human capital, skills development, mentorship, and economic opportunity creation.

Our Founder

Boaz Selemani transforming lived experience into lasting change

Boaz founded FAAF in 2020 at age 20. Born in the DRC and raised in Dzaleka Refugee Camp, he experienced aid dependency firsthand and refused to accept it as permanent reality. Today he leads FAAF through servant leadership, building systems that outlast individual involvement.

Our Mission

Ending aid dependency

We invest in human capital, skills development, mentorship, and economic opportunity creation to transform communities from aid dependency to sustainable self-reliance.

Who we serve

FAAF works with young people, children at risk, women, refugees, persons with disabilities, and households with limited opportunity across Malawi.

What we believe

Aid without capability creates dependency. FAAF builds capability, dignity, and local leadership so communities can become self-reliant over time.

Why this matters

The problem is not a lack of potential; it is the absence of systems, mentorship, and opportunity that convert potential into progress.

Our Work

From aid to ability in practice

FAAF's work is rooted in the lived experience of displacement, uncertainty, and the need for practical solutions. We help communities grow from receiving support to generating capability, resilience, and opportunity for the next generation.

Our Approach

From aid to ability

FAAF works by combining skills, mentorship, opportunity, and local leadership. We do not only deliver one off support, we build systems that help people move from dependency to agency.

1. Discover

We listen to the realities, gaps, and aspirations in communities before designing interventions.

2. Build

We create practical pathways for livelihoods, mentorship, and leadership development.

3. Sustain

We strengthen local structures so progress continues long after the initial initiative ends.
