Our Values

The principles that guide everything we do

FAAF is guided by five core values that shape our programs, our partnerships, and our commitment to sustainable development.

Core Values

What we stand for

Ability

We measure success by what people can do, not by what they receive. Every program must answer: What new capability does the participant possess at graduation?

Dignity

We consult communities during design, not merely inform them after implementation. We never use exploitative imagery for fundraising. We protect participant data with the same rigor as financial records. We address people by their preferred names and titles.

Independence

Our goal is to work ourselves out of a job in every community we serve. We celebrate when communities no longer need us.

Integrity

Every dollar is traceable to outcome. We report failures with the same honesty as successes. We maintain zero tolerance for harassment, corruption, or discrimination.

Community Transformation

Individual success must translate to collective benefit. We measure community-level indicators, not just individual stories.

Our Approach

What makes FAAF different

FAAF does not simply deliver services. We build capacity, measure outcomes, and design every intervention with a planned exit strategy.

Conventional Approach FAAF Approach
Deliver services to beneficiaries Build capacity with partners
Measure outputs (people trained, workshops held) Measure outcomes (income generated, dependency reduced, businesses sustained)
Permanent organizational presence Planned organizational exit with community ownership
Generic, one-size-fits-all programs Market-responsive, context-specific design based on local labor market analysis
Gender programs focus exclusively on women and girls Gender programs empower women while addressing boys' mentorship as complementary investment
External funding dependency Progressive local resource mobilization from year one
Crisis response without sustainability planning Every intervention includes sustainability roadmap and graduation criteria

Our Vision

A Malawi where ability replaces aid

We envision a Malawi where youth unemployment is addressed through market-driven skills and enterprise creation, where women exercise full economic agency, where children grow up in environments where "I can't" is replaced with "I can learn," and where communities possess the internal capacity to identify challenges, mobilize resources, and implement solutions.

Development organizations should measure success by their own redundancy, not by their institutional growth.
